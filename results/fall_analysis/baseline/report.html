What does failure look like?

Final frame of 9 falls (sorted by score, worst first) and 3 episodes that ran out of time (worst, median, best). 27 of 300 episodes end in a fall; every other episode ends at the 1600-step limit before the end of the course.

Where does it happen?

Left: falls are spread along the course, not tied to one place. Middle and right: hull angle against forward speed. Normal walking stays in a tight spot; before a fall the hull pitches nose-down while still moving forward.

What drifts before failure?

Median (line) and interquartile band over the last 300 steps of every fall. Dashed: the typical value during normal walking. Blue = inputs the network already sees, orange = candidate signals it doesn't.

What predicts failure?

Left: AUC of each signal alone for "falls within 50 steps" on snapshots every 10 steps (the network has no value estimate, so the dashed line is the best single input, hull angle). Right: correlation with time-to-fall after removing what the 24 inputs already explain. A high bar here is information the network does not have.

What causes it?

Left: the direction of the fall from the final hull angle. Middle: terrain slope under the hull in the last second before falls, compared with all steps. Right: how many feet are on the ground in the last second, compared with normal.

How does it start?

Three falls (short, median and long run-up): the last healthy step (hull angle and pitch rate both within the normal 75th percentile), two steps in between, and the fall. Median distance from onset to fall: 14 steps (0.3 s); middle half 8-24 steps.
